How to read the wound bed. Each fibroblast mark has a fixed position and is drawn only where the model's local density n/K exceeds a threshold assigned to that mark, so the share of marks present at any distance from the centre is the computed density — the picture is a plot, not an illustration. The blue dotted line on the density plot is the most the current drug level can sustain; once it sits below the orange closure line, the wound cannot close however long you wait. Static versions: Figures 11 and 14. In Patches mode each patch is the 2.5 mm alginate patch of Interactive 3, which keeps tissue above the 80.9 µM target for about 11.8 h; the drug effect then fades with an 18 h time constant until the next patch (the blue band on the timeline; its solid line is the 1-day average, which is what has to stay above 33 %). The longest interval that still closes is about 36.5 h, so a once-daily change is inside the envelope; at longer intervals the front advances and then stalls. Every rate comes from the Tier-4 closure functions of the constants register (chronic rp = 0.50, d = 0.51 d−1; treated rp = 1.11, d = 0.45 d−1). The animation stops when the front reaches the centre, because the model describes a chronic wound bed.
